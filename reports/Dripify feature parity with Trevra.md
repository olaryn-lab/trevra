# Trevra out-sequences Dripify but starves at intake

In October 2026 Dripify is a fast-shipping cloud sequencer for LinkedIn and email. It sells at **$79 or $99 per LinkedIn seat per month**, and its $59 Basic tier disappeared between 3 and 20 October 2026. Trevra already matches or beats Dripify on the sequencing engine itself, but trails badly on what feeds and surrounds that engine. Across the 46 capabilities compared below, Trevra is **ahead on 16, at parity on 8, partial on 6, behind on 11, and absent by design on 5**. Trevra leads on conditional logic, A/B testing, multi-sender throughput control, a stricter deterministic safety gate, a cross-channel inbox, an MCP surface that cannot send, and an exact-payload approval ledger with no Dripify equivalent. It trails on lead sourcing (LinkedIn search, Sales Navigator and post-engager import are built but hard-disabled), cloud execution (Trevra runs in the member's own Chrome), AI writing help, CRM push and webhooks, per-rep analytics, inbox organization, and a working trial and billing flow. Several code defects undercut the parity claims. Email replies are never counted. Email sends are never counted as "messaged". Email reply and bounce conditions never fire. Campaign email ships without the unsubscribe header and footer that Trevra's own first-touch path enforces. Trevra's internal parity documents are stale about both products. The market argues against copying Dripify feature for feature. Dripify's core value is cloud automation that LinkedIn's User Agreement §8.2 prohibits, its price band is being pushed down toward $7–$20 a seat, and LinkedIn has escalated from removing vendor pages to suing scrapers. Trevra should fix the defects and bring campaign email inside its compliance gates first. It should then close the intake, drafting, integration and team-reporting gaps through approval-gated, credential-safe routes, and keep refusing cloud LinkedIn automation, scraping and fingerprint evasion.

## Dripify ships every month but is still a per-seat LinkedIn tool

Dripify is a privately held, remote, mostly Ukrainian company. CB Insights places it in Kyiv, founded in 2019 ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/dripify)). Its careers site claims "Founded in 2017", "100+" coworkers and "40,000+ companies" ([Dripify careers](https://career.dripify.com/people)), while its pricing page claims "thousands of companies in 72+ countries" ([Pricing](https://dripify.com/pricing/)). Its terms name no legal entity, registered address or governing law ([Terms](https://dripify.com/terms-and-conditions/)), and no funding is disclosed. On 20 October 2026 the pricing page listed **Pro at $79 per seat per month ($59 billed annually), Advanced at $99 ($79 annually), and custom Enterprise**. Each seat connects one LinkedIn account: "A team of five reps needs five seats" ([Pricing](https://dripify.com/pricing/)). Basic was still listed at $59 on 3 October ([Wayback 2026-10-03](https://web.archive.org/web/20261003065935/https://dripify.com/pricing/)) and was gone by 20 October. Removing it **raised the cheapest entry by about 34% on monthly billing and 51% on annual billing**, even though headline prices have not changed since July 2022 ([Wayback 2022-07-25](https://web.archive.org/web/20220725151007/https://dripify.io/pricing/)). Dripify now makes more money by moving features between tiers, not by raising list prices. A/B testing was a Pro feature in 2022 and is Advanced-only today. The "up to 35% off" annual discount was only true for Basic: by the listed prices, Pro now saves about 25% and Advanced about 20%. The trial lasts 7 days, needs no card and runs on Advanced. Monthly plans cannot be refunded, and annual plans only within 24 hours of payment ([Terms](https://dripify.com/terms-and-conditions/)).

The 2026 changelog shows a team catching up quickly on email, team structure and developer access:

- **May:** post-engager sourcing with Evergreen auto-refresh.
- **July:** LinkedIn Groups as a lead source, multiple webhook triggers per campaign, Sales Navigator InMail (which replaced Basic InMail), and email reply detection that pauses the lead in every campaign ([July 2026 update](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/15902276-product-update-july-2026)).
- **August:** 50 custom lead fields, bounce suppression, email step analytics, an Unfollow action, A/B testing for messages, and a webhook that carries the full LinkedIn conversation ([August 2026 update](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/16384791-product-update-august-2026)).
- **September:** an Open API and A/B testing for email and invites ([September 2026 update](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/16938124-product-update-september-2026)).
- **Late September:** a remote MCP server with four tools ([MCP](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/17171745-how-to-connect-dripify-to-ai-assistants-with-the-mcp-server)) and "Improve with AI", a rewrite tool free up to **150 uses a month** ([Improve with AI](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/17060910-how-to-improve-your-messages-with-ai)).

The sequence builder offers about a dozen actions, four conditions that are each checked once, delays in hours or days, live editing of running campaigns, and 18 or more pre-built templates filtered by lead source ([Actions](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/5028711-what-actions-can-be-added-to-your-sequence); [Conditions](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/6788403-how-to-use-conditions); [Templates](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/12478338-how-to-use-templates-in-dripify)). Daily caps on Sales Navigator accounts reach **75 invites and 150 messages**, with a randomizing "Range" setting and adaptive "Activity Control" on Advanced ([Daily limits](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/8201637-how-dripify-daily-limits-safeguard-your-linkedin-account)).

What Dripify still lacks says as much as what it has:

- **Inbox:** it shows only LinkedIn conversations with leads. Email replies, Sales Navigator InMail replies and non-lead threads do not appear ([Inbox overview](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/5030193-dripify-inbox-overview); [Email replies](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/15502759-how-dripify-handles-email-replies)).
- **CRM:** the only native CRM is a HubSpot beta that pushes one way and, per its own help page, "new contacts aren't created in HubSpot yet" ([HubSpot](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/16198748-how-to-set-up-hubspot-integration)).
- **Data and agencies:** it has no data or intent signals of its own; "high-intent leads" is still a "Notify me" item on the homepage ([Homepage](https://dripify.com/)). Its own comparison table marks sender rotation and white label as "No" ([HeyReach vs Dripify](https://dripify.com/comparison/heyreach-vs-dripify/)).
- **Analytics:** reporting stops at the reply. There is no pipeline or revenue attribution ([Analytics FAQ](https://dripify.com/features/advanced-analytics/)).
- **AI:** the AI is a copy editor that works "based on your original text" without reading lead data ([Improve with AI](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/17060910-how-to-improve-your-messages-with-ai)).

Dripify's marketing pages contradict its help center on variable counts, InMail caps, HubSpot contact creation, and whether email appears in the inbox. This report therefore pins Dripify claims to dated help-center and changelog articles, not marketing copy.

## Dripify sells a safety promise that LinkedIn's rules prohibit

Dripify runs campaigns on its own AWS-hosted servers ([Trust Center](https://dripify.com/trust-center/)). It gives each account a "dedicated" IP address in the user's region and spaces actions with randomized timing inside working hours ([Pricing FAQ](https://dripify.com/pricing/)). Users connect LinkedIn either by typing their email and password into Dripify or through a Chrome extension that reuses the logged-in browser session ([Three steps](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/4909971-three-steps-to-start-using-dripify); [Chrome extension](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/17094091-how-to-connect-your-linkedin-account-using-the-dripify-chrome-extension)). The customer privacy policy lists "social networking cookies" among the data it collects ([Customer privacy policy](https://dripify.com/privacy-policy/customer/)).

LinkedIn prohibits exactly this. Its help center bans "any third party software ... that ... automate activity on LinkedIn's website". User Agreement §8.2 forbids "bots or other unauthorized automated methods" that "send or redirect messages", and members who use them "risk having their accounts restricted or shut down" ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387)). Dripify's help center still calls the product "completely safe to use" and promises measures "to ensure you won't get flagged" ([Dripify Help](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/5030328-how-can-i-be-sure-my-account-won-t-get-blocked-by-linkedin)). Its paid masterclass menu includes a **"Step-by-step guide on creating a bot account on LinkedIn"** and "safe ways to bypass" the weekly invite limit ([Pricing](https://dripify.com/pricing/)). Its LinkedIn company page was named with Cyrillic look-alike letters ("Drіріfу") in 2025 snapshots and now returns 404 ([Wayback 2025-09-03](https://web.archive.org/web/20250903132612/https://www.linkedin.com/company/sales-automation-software/)). Why the page disappeared is unconfirmed. If an account is restricted, Dripify's remedy is support help plus extending the subscription for the days lost ([Avoid blocks](https://help.dripify.com/en/articles/12821690-how-to-avoid-being-blocked-or-flagged-by-linkedin)).

Dripify's compliance posture is self-declared. The Trust Center claims GDPR-aligned processing, AES-256 encryption at rest, TLS 1.2/1.3 in transit and SCC-based transfers. The DPA and the subprocessor list are available only on request, and no SOC 2 or ISO 27001 certification is claimed ([Trust Center](https://dripify.com/trust-center/)). Data sits outside the EU, including the United States, and the customer carries the burden of a lawful basis for scraped lead data ([Customer privacy policy](https://dripify.com/privacy-policy/customer/)). The Chrome Web Store lists Dripify as a "Non-trader", so EU consumer rights do not apply ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/dripify/lmikkmkgoflkjcjhalfbmaocanmmfffm)). An EU or mid-market buyer has little contractual recourse.

Aggregate user ratings are high but probably inflated by solicited reviews:

- **Capterra: 4.7 from 477 reviews** ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/215082/Dripify/reviews/)).
- **G2: 4.5 from about 354 reviews.** This comes from a search snippet; G2 blocked direct fetching ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/dripify/reviews)).
- **Trustpilot: about 4.5–4.6 from roughly 490–530 reviews,** also from snippets ([Trustpilot UK](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/dripify.com?page=3)).

Reviewers praise easy setup, cloud execution ("laptop can be shut down as Dripify runs in the background") and responsive support. Complaints cluster around:

- price versus the volume actually delivered ("very expensive for a start-up");
- the old Basic plan's single campaign that could not be edited;
- auto-renewal, including an unannounced £473 annual renewal refunded only after escalation ([Trustpilot UK p.7](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/dripify.com?page=7));
- a dated UI and a leads-only inbox;
- needing Sales Navigator to target anyone ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/215082/Dripify/reviews/)).

Capterra reviews cluster in January–April 2025, at least one top review is labeled "Incentivized", and Dripify replies to almost every review. Restriction rates for Dripify users come only from competitors (about 40% of accounts in Q1 2026; 27–34% after a supposed May 2026 enforcement change) and are unverified ([Getcleed](https://www.getcleed.com/blog/linkedin-automation-crackdown-2026); [HotHawk](https://hothawk.ai/compare/dripify-alternatives)). The ratings best reflect satisfaction during onboarding. The lasting pain shows up as restrictions and billing friction, and both are openings for a rival that does not automate the user's LinkedIn identity in the cloud.

## Dripify sits in the most commoditized of four market groups

The category has split into four groups:

1. Cheap LinkedIn-only sequencers.
2. Cloud tools priced per sender with heavy safety engineering.
3. Multichannel engagement suites.
4. AI-SDR and signal or workflow platforms that treat LinkedIn as optional.

Dripify sits between the first two groups. The table shows list prices fetched on 2026-10-20.

The table stakes are now: cloud execution with a per-account IP, Sales Navigator import, if/then sequences, safety caps and warm-up, a unified inbox, CRM sync or Zapier, an API, and multi-account management. Dripify's 2026 releases bring it to that baseline. The newer battleground is AI reply handling with a human-approval mode, post and intent signals, bundled data, MCP interfaces and flat-fee unlimited senders. Skylead, lemlist, HeyReach and Expandi each shipped one or more of these in 2026, while Dripify's signals and AI personalization remain announcements. Matching Dripify feature for feature only buys parity with a segment where a seat costs between $7 and $100, and it inherits the same terms-of-service exposure.

That exposure is structural, because **no sanctioned way exists to automate LinkedIn invitations or messages**. LinkedIn's Messages API is open only to approved partners. Every message must come from "a specific member action" (scheduled or automated events do not count), the member must be able to edit the draft and take an affirmative action to send, and recipients are first-degree connections only ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/integrations/communications/messages)). The Sales Navigator partner API (SNAP) is "not currently accepting new partners" ([Microsoft Learn: SNAP](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/sales/)). HeyReach states it plainly: "There is no approved outreach automation tool — ours included" ([HeyReach](https://www.heyreach.io/blog/heyreach-ban)).

Enforcement has escalated:

- **Proxycurl:** sued in January 2025 and shut down by July 2025 ([Nubela](https://nubela.co/blog/goodbye-proxycurl/)).
- **ProAPIs:** sued in October 2025 ([Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/183001/security/linkedin-sues-proapis-for-15k-month-linkedin-data-scraping-scheme.html)).
- **Apollo and Seamless.AI:** company pages removed in March 2025 ([MarTech](https://martech.org/a-pair-of-lead-gen-providers-have-disappeared-from-linkedin/)).
- **Artisan:** restricted in December 2025, then reinstated ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/07/yes-linkedin-banned-ai-agent-startup-artisan-but-now-its-back)).
- **HeyReach:** company page removed and four executives' profiles restricted on 25 March 2026 ([HeyReach](https://www.heyreach.io/blog/heyreach-ban)).
- **Extension scanning:** LinkedIn's page scripts were found probing for **6,236 browser extensions** ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/linkedin-secretly-scans-for-6-000-plus-chrome-extensions-collects-data)).

The Fairlinked antitrust class action filed on 14 September 2026 challenges this regime but is unresolved ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/linkedin-faces-antitrust-class-action-over-sales-navigator-10-license-minimum/)). Planning should assume the current rules hold through 2027. LinkedIn is also building its own "Sales Assistant" that delivers daily leads and outreach drafts ([LinkedIn Sales community](https://scommunity.linkedin.com/new-sales-navigator-features-64/introducing-sales-assistant-your-best-leads-delivered-daily-7653)). The fully autonomous AI-SDR pitch has lost credibility: ZoomInfo said 11x "performed significantly worse than our SDR employees" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/24/a16z-and-benchmark-backed-11x-has-been-claiming-customers-it-doesnt-have/)).

The direction is agent-operated and human-governed outreach. None of the tools reviewed offers deterministic approval of exact outbound payloads, evidence attached to each action, or an auditable record of who approved what. Skylead's semi-automatic mode is the closest. That empty space is where Trevra's architecture already sits.

Trevra's leads matter less commercially than its gaps. **Dripify's onboarding is "paste a Sales Navigator search URL and press start". Trevra answers that request with a 409.** Trevra's hosted path also needs a Companion app running on an always-on computer the member owns. Together these mean a buyer coming from Dripify would hit a wall in the first hour, even though Trevra's sequencing, safety and inbox are stronger once leads exist.

Refusing scraping is deliberate, and the landscape supports it. LinkedIn sued Proxycurl and ProAPIs over scraping, and France's CNIL fined KASPR €240,000 for collecting contact data from LinkedIn ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/en/data-scraping-kaspr-fined-eu240000)). The gap still needs a compliant answer, not just a refusal.

Trevra's own LinkedIn automation is not cleared under LinkedIn's terms either. The Companion uses Playwright to drive the member's real Chrome over the Chrome DevTools Protocol, and `docs/core-product.md:145-150` names §8.2 exposure and selector rot as the "honest costs". The Companion lowers fingerprint risk and keeps sessions off Trevra's servers. Only human-send mode approaches the pattern LinkedIn's Messages API rules describe: a prepared draft, then an affirmative send by the member.

The approval boundary is also applied unevenly. LinkedIn actions and campaign email run outside the closed set of four execution adapters (`src/server/control-plane/execution.ts:20-31`). Their only approval is starting the campaign (plus optional human-send), and the workspace policy engine is never evaluated for them. No model sits in that loop, so this is consistent with the spirit of "deterministic software controls external execution". It is not consistent with the letter of AGENTS.md, which requires a dedicated prepared-action adapter and the approval/execution boundary for external writes.

Pricing structure is a latent advantage that Trevra cannot yet sell. Five LinkedIn seats on Dripify Advanced cost **$395 a month billed annually or $495 monthly**, before Sales Navigator. Trevra's preview Growth plan is $149 per workspace with no seat count. But Trevra enforces no entitlements, has no trial, and disables plan changes (`src/client/BillingSettings.tsx:253-263`), so the advantage exists only on paper.

The P0-2 design choice deserves care. Requiring approval of every rendered campaign email would destroy the cadence value of sequences. Approving nothing per send leaves campaign email outside the boundary AGENTS.md describes. The proposed middle path keeps the boundary deterministic:

1. Campaign start approves the template, the audience snapshot and the policy.
2. The adapter re-checks suppression, lawful basis, footer and unsubscribe at send time.
3. Each rendered payload hash is recorded before execution.

This is close to how `email.send` already re-checks at send time (`src/server/control-plane/execution.ts:51-134`). Bringing LinkedIn runner actions under the same policy evaluation would also make Trevra's governance claim uniform rather than selective.

P1-6 is the strongest product move. Dripify's AI only polishes text the user wrote. LinkedIn's own sanctioned pattern requires an editable draft and an affirmative member send. Trevra already has the pieces: evidence-backed research, a hash-locked approval engine, and a human-send mode. Wiring them together produces something no Dripify-class tool offers. That workflow also lines up with the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency duties, which have applied since 2 August 2026 ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-08-03-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-take-effect-2-august-2026)). Whether AI-drafted, human-sent sales messages need labelling is not settled. A further judgment call, with a throughput cost, is to make human-send the default for invites and DMs on new hosted seats and keep automated sending as an informed opt-in. That would put Trevra's default on the safest side of LinkedIn's rules while enforcement keeps escalating.

Some Dripify capabilities should stay absent by design:

- **Server-side cloud automation of members' LinkedIn identities on dedicated IPs or proxies.** This is the behavior §8.2 targets.
- **Synthetic typing, mouse movement and fingerprint spoofing.** These exist to evade detection.
- **Scraping LinkedIn search, Sales Navigator, or post engagers at scale.** This is the conduct LinkedIn has sued over and regulators have fined.
- **Rented profiles, and campaign types marketed as bypassing invite limits.**
- **Aggregator connections such as Unipile.** Reliable reconnection there depends on storing users' passwords and 2FA secrets ([Unipile](https://developer.unipile.com/docs/linkedin)), which conflicts with AGENTS.md's credential rule.

Trevra should position against Dripify around these refusals rather than hide them. The likeliest switchers are founders burned by restrictions or billing, and Trevra's public site currently mentions LinkedIn exactly once (`public/pricing/index.html:119`).

## Conclusion

The comparison reverses the usual competitive question. Dripify is a thin engine on top of a broad funnel: sourcing in a single paste, cloud execution, CRM and webhook push, and per-rep dashboards for managers. Trevra is a deep engine on top of a narrow funnel. Its runner already exceeds Dripify on branching, throughput, safety and auditability, but few leads reach it, and the email leg's analytics and compliance contradict Trevra's own standards. Parity work should therefore flow outward from the engine, through intake, drafting and integrations, rather than adding more actions to a sequencer that is already overbuilt for its inputs.

The deeper risk is internal consistency, not Dripify. Trevra's sharpest differentiator is exact-payload approval governed by deterministic policy. Today it covers first-touch email, community replies and Buffer drafts, while the two channels a Dripify buyer actually uses, LinkedIn actions and campaign email, run beside it. In a market where LinkedIn's own API rules, Gmail enforcement, EU AI Act transparency duties and the failures of autonomous AI SDRs all push toward human-governed outreach, Trevra wins by making that governance universal and visible. A 34% entry-price increase at Dripify, a restriction-burned user base, and no rival offering approval ledgers make October 2026 an unusually good window to do it.
