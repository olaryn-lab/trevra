# Trevra Must Observe More and Own Less

> **Historical research snapshot — 6 October 2026.** Competitor pricing, product capabilities, code locations, security boundaries and the proposed GTM roadmap reflect evidence available at that date. This is decision-support research, not a current implementation inventory or an approved execution plan; revalidate against the latest `main` and provider documentation before relying on individual claims.

Trevra already does the hardest and most differentiated part of what LemonCrow and Beseam need: account signals, developer-community listening, evidence-checked drafting, LinkedIn execution under code-owned safety limits, and approvals bound to the exact payload. It is missing five capabilities that both companies' main motions depend on: **product-milestone signals** (LemonCrow installs and activations; Beseam's scan-to-store-connected funnel), **email as a standalone governed channel** with deliverability checks and inbound-mailbox sync, **meetings**, **jurisdiction-aware consent**, and **tracking of the founder's own AI-search visibility**. The two companies name 35 GTM jobs. Mapped onto a 28-function GTM taxonomy, Trevra fully covers 4 functions, partly covers 11, lacks 7, and deliberately excludes 5: pricing decisions, quotes, billing, onboarding and retention. Neither company needs the remaining one, phone calls. "Every need" should not mean "own everything". Trevra's own admission rule says a connector must either observe GTM state or execute a named GTM action ([integration-contracts.md](../docs/integration-contracts.md)). That rule is the way to cover product, billing and CRM state without rebuilding the post-sale domain it deleted on purpose. The 2026 market backs this route. Founders stitch together roughly $400/month of Clay, Instantly, HeyReach and Attio, credit burn and complexity are the most repeated complaints, and no compared vendor documents payload-bound approvals ([Clay](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). Simplicity comes from the same discipline: five verbs, one Today queue, fixed atoms, connected sources instead of configuration, and approvals tiered by blast radius. Users approve **93%** of uniform permission prompts, so more prompts do not mean more safety ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode)). The largest material risk is concentration. Trevra's deepest subsystem automates LinkedIn, whose User Agreement prohibits automated messaging ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)), and one founder profile carries GTM for both companies.

## Two companies, 35 jobs, one shape: free proof, product milestone, human close

On the surface LemonCrow and Beseam are unrelated. One is a developer tool that cuts AI-agent cost and reviews agent-written diffs. The other is software that finds where Shopify products go missing in AI shopping answers. Their GTM skeletons are nearly identical, though.

**LemonCrow** is free and Apache-2.0, with **no paid plans**, retired Pro subscriptions, and a hosted tier described as **"not built yet"** ([LemonCrow pricing](https://lemoncrow.com/pricing)). Its visible traction is **72 GitHub stars and 5 forks** across 993 commits ([lemoncrow-lab](https://github.com/lemoncrow-lab)). Its proof is benchmark data: on SWE-bench Verified, LemonCrow-run agents cost **29.5% less**, ran **23.7% faster**, and resolved **92.8% of tasks versus 80.8%** without it, and the published results include a Lite regression ([GitHub README](https://github.com/lemoncrow-lab/lemoncrow)). Its only path to money is design-partner pilots for hosted and customer-VPC deployments, and the controls for those are "not all generally available yet" ([LemonCrow enterprise](https://lemoncrow.com/enterprise)).

**Beseam** publishes no pricing; its `/pricing` URL returned 404 on 6 October 2026 ([beseam.com/pricing](https://beseam.com/pricing)). Prospects come in through a **free one-minute public-domain scan** and a **20-minute store review** ([Beseam scan](https://beseam.com/de/scan), [Beseam contact](https://beseam.com/contact)). Its owned content asset is an AI Shopping Report: across 45 answers from ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Mode, **71% of 153 brand namings came from only one engine** ([Beseam benchmarks](https://beseam.com/benchmarks)). Both companies are founder-led and early. Neither has published a customer case study, and Beseam labels its examples "not customer results" ([Beseam platform](https://beseam.com/platform)).

The shared shape runs in four moves: free self-serve proof (a curl install or a domain scan), then a product milestone that shows intent (a first `lc review`, or a connected Shopify store), then a human conversation (a design-partner call, or the store review), and only then payment.

The Beseam research profile derives this pipeline: _scanned, account created, Shopify connected, first approved change, re-check shown, paid_. **Four of those six stages are product events.** LemonCrow's natural activation event (first review, or first session stats) is measured by on-by-default anonymous telemetry. The inference is that **for both companies the real pipeline is a product-milestone pipeline**. Trevra's sales-stage pipeline (new, qualified, meeting, proposal, won/lost) sees only its last third ([PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md)).

Both brands also make the promise Trevra makes. Beseam's loop is Find, Prepare, Approve, Apply, Measure, with "no unsupervised agent" ([Beseam manifesto](https://beseam.com/manifesto), [how we work](https://beseam.com/how-we-work)). LemonCrow sells better context, execution, evidence and human judgment around agents ([lemoncrow.com](https://lemoncrow.com)). Trevra's invariant applies the same ethic to GTM: decision, prepared exact action, payload hash, policy, human approval, deterministic adapter, then outcome ([PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md)). For these two users the governance layer is not overhead; it is the brand. An agent that invented a security certification or emailed an existing customer would contradict exactly what both companies sell. Trevra was also partly built from their needs. The community-outreach scout ships with keywords for "AI coding agent cost" threads, ported from LemonCrow's tree ([community-outreach.md](../docs/community-outreach.md)). Beseam's landing-page Cloudflare Worker is designed to post leads into Trevra's intake ([lead-capture spec](../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-20-generic-lead-capture-design.md)).

## Trevra already owns the differentiated, hard-to-copy core

Most of what is hard to build is already there. For Beseam, the **storefront crawler detects Shopify and WooCommerce and reads public catalogs**. The signal vocabulary includes storefront rebuilds, commerce apps added or removed, Meta ads started or rising, hiring changes and pricing changes ([storefront crawler](../src/server/storefront/crawler.ts), [signal kinds](../src/server/accounts/types.ts)). The `gtm.visibility-audit` skill probes a prospect domain for `llms.txt`, AI-bot robots rules, product feeds, sitemaps and structured data. It already grounds the `gtm.audit-led-outreach` playbook ([audit.ts](../src/server/skills/audit.ts), [playbook registry](../src/server/playbooks/registry.ts)). That is Beseam's "pre-outreach scan run" job in all but name. Contact finding crawls same-origin `/contact`, `/about`, `/team` and `/imprint` pages for published addresses and keeps guessed addresses off by default ([contact.ts](../src/server/skills/contact.ts)). That suits DACH sites that publish an imprint page and avoids buying lists, which Google's sender guidelines tell senders not to do ([Google](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126)).

For LemonCrow, **brand and keyword watches run daily or weekly across Hacker News, Stack Overflow, Lobsters, GitHub, Reddit and Mastodon**. Replies go through a `gtm.thread-reply` playbook (guard, draft, approve, post), with automatic posting only on GitHub and Mastodon and manual handoff elsewhere ([playbook registry](../src/server/playbooks/registry.ts), [publish.ts](../src/server/outreach/publish.ts)). The scout caps Reddit at 5 replies a day with a 48-hour cooldown and Hacker News at 3 a day, and it limits self-promotion to a 0.1 ratio ([outreach config](../src/server/outreach/config.ts)). That posture fits developer communities, where bot-like promotion destroys credibility.

Content drafting includes a deterministic proof critic and an evidence map. Channel adapters publish by API only to Bluesky, dev.to, GitHub and Mastodon; they prepare HN, Product Hunt, X, LinkedIn and others for human handoff ([channel adapters](../src/server/channels/adapters/)). For founders whose brands rest on honest benchmarks, "draft-first, publish by hand on the platforms that punish automation" is the right default, not a missing feature.

The governance core is genuinely rare. The payload hash is SHA-256 over key-sorted stable JSON, so editing a prepared payload invalidates its approval ([payload.ts](../src/server/control-plane/payload.ts)). The execution registry is closed to four named actions: `email.send`, `community.reply`, `crm.log-activity` and `buffer.create-draft` ([execution.ts](../src/server/control-plane/execution.ts)). Agent tokens carry no approve or execute scope ([agent-access.ts](../src/server/agent-access.ts)). The 13-tool MCP endpoint lets the founder drive Trevra from Claude Code or Codex ([mcp-http.ts](../src/server/mcp-http.ts)), which suits a founder who already runs LemonCrow inside those hosts. LinkedIn execution enforces warm-up and steady-state bands (for example **10 invites a day and 20 a week during warm-up; 18 a day and 90 a week steady**) through 15 guard checks and a suppression list shared across email and LinkedIn ([limits.ts](../src/server/linkedin/limits.ts), [guard.ts](../src/server/linkedin/guard.ts)). These are the pieces a competitor cannot retrofit cheaply. Every gap below should be built on them, not around them.

## Five structural gaps sit under both companies' pipelines

### Product milestones are excluded, yet they define both funnels

The canonical spec lists "arbitrary product-event ingestion" under _What not to build_ ([agent-native spec](../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-20-agent-native-gtm-os-design.md)). Opportunity-lite has six fixed stages and no amount ([opportunities.ts](../src/server/opportunities.ts)). As a result, neither LemonCrow's install-to-activation funnel nor Beseam's scan-to-connect funnel can create, rank or advance anything in Trevra. A grep of `src/` for `stargazer` returns nothing, so GitHub repo traction (stars, forks, release downloads) is absent too. That is LemonCrow's main visible signal, even though OSS-growth vendors claim only about **1 to 3% of stargazers are buyers** (an unverified vendor figure, [Clarm](https://www.clarm.com/blog/articles/convert-github-stars-to-revenue)).

The exclusion was written against _arbitrary_ events, and the fix keeps that word meaningful. Trevra should add a **closed vocabulary of GTM milestones**, such as `signed_up`, `activated`, `integration_connected`, `usage_threshold_crossed`, `paid` and `cancelled`. They would arrive through the same HMAC-signed intake that lead capture already uses ([lead-capture http.ts](../src/server/lead-capture/http.ts)), carry no content, and attach as signals to a Person or Account. Each milestone would advance or create an Opportunity deterministically, just as high-intent inbound kinds already auto-create Opportunities ([demand/opportunities.ts](../src/server/demand/opportunities.ts)). This fits the invariant that "signals must have GTM meaning". It also respects AGENTS.md's rule that analytics carry no client content or IP addresses ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)). The tension to name is the privacy one: LemonCrow telemetry is anonymous and hashed, so only installs that the user deliberately links to an identity (sign-in, `lc team`) can become person-level signals. That is a feature, not a limitation to engineer around.

### Email rides as a passenger inside LinkedIn campaigns

LemonCrow's buyers are staff engineers, platform teams and engineering managers, and for them email is the natural outbound channel. Trevra, however, runs email only as steps _inside_ LinkedIn-managed campaigns ([workflows.ts](../src/server/linkedin/workflows.ts)), and the intent compiler refuses other channels with `prepare_outreach_linkedin_only` ([intent.ts](../src/server/gtm/intent.ts)). The sending layer underneath is thin as well: there is **no mailbox rotation, no domain warm-up and no SPF/DKIM/DMARC check** ([campaign-channels.ts](../src/server/linkedin/campaign-channels.ts)), and a case-insensitive grep of `src/` and `packages/` for `List-Unsubscribe` returns no files.

Google requires SPF or DKIM, valid PTR records, TLS and a spam rate **under 0.3%** from all senders. Above 5,000 messages a day it adds DMARC alignment and one-click unsubscribe, and it recommends **staying under 0.10%** ([Google](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126)). Founder volumes sit far below 5,000 a day, but a bad sending domain hurts every company that shares it. The idempotent delivery states, the 50-a-day default cap, and suppression on bounce and unsubscribe are good foundations ([email-deliveries.ts](../src/server/email-deliveries.ts), [email-outcomes.ts](../src/server/email-outcomes.ts)). What is missing is email as a peer channel in the compiler, plus deliverability checks surfaced as plan blockers.

### The inbound front door has no mailbox and no meeting

LemonCrow's whole enterprise funnel is an email address ([LemonCrow enterprise](https://lemoncrow.com/enterprise)). Trevra captures inbound mail only by polling threads it sent itself, so **unsolicited inbound mail does not become a conversation** ([campaign-channels.ts](../src/server/linkedin/campaign-channels.ts)). A Nango `message` record path exists, but whether deployed Gmail syncs feed it was not verified ([integration-service.ts](../src/server/integration-service.ts)). Beseam's conversion step is a 20-minute store review with a one-business-day response promise. Trevra's Google Calendar card reads **"Not ready yet — Trevra has nowhere to put meetings, so connecting this brings nothing in"** ([integration-service.ts](../src/server/integration-service.ts)), and `meeting` exists only as an Opportunity stage set by hand.

Signed intake is built but not live for Beseam: "live Capture Source provisioning/verification is pending", and "Remove SendPulse/local website lead state" is still unchecked ([agent-native spec](../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-20-agent-native-gtm-os-design.md)). LemonCrow has no edge function yet, and the intake secret must never sit in browser JavaScript ([lead-capture.md](../docs/lead-capture.md)). So an embeddable or hosted form is the missing piece on that side.

Meetings are the most conspicuous gap the product's own model implies. They need no new screen. Calendar events can land as conversation activities and move the Opportunity to `meeting`. A booking link can be a merge field in drafts. A prep brief is simply the output of the `research_accounts` objective, which today is plan-only ([intent.ts](../src/server/gtm/intent.ts)).

### Consent is a global suppression list, not a jurisdiction rule

Beseam sells into Switzerland and the DACH region, and LemonCrow sells globally. The rules diverge sharply. Germany's UWG §7 is reported to require prior consent even for B2B email ([Overloop](https://overloop.com/blog/b2b-cold-email-germany-gdpr-compliance), a vendor source to verify with counsel). Switzerland's nFADP, in force since 1 September 2023, includes a right to object to direct marketing ([KMU portal](https://www.kmu.admin.ch/en/new-federal-act-on-data-protection-nfadp)). CAN-SPAM covers B2B email and requires a physical address and an opt-out honored within 10 business days ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/can-spam-penalties), secondary).

Trevra enforces suppression deterministically ([global suppressions](../migrations/101_global_gtm_suppressions.sql)), but it records no lawful basis, channel consent or recipient jurisdiction. It has no check that the sender's own identity is complete either. That last check matters now: Beseam's `/terms`, `/privacy` and `/legal/privacy` all returned 404 on 6 October 2026, according to the research profile. Outbound should not start until a privacy notice exists. A deterministic policy condition could block any send whose recipient country requires consent the workspace lacks, or whose sender record has no postal address or privacy URL. That is exactly the class of rule the policy engine was built for ([policy.ts](../src/server/control-plane/policy.ts)).

### Nobody tracks the founder's own AI-search visibility

Trevra serves `llms.txt`, `agents.md`, a sitemap and JSON-LD for its _own_ site ([public-site.ts](../src/server/public-site.ts)). Its visibility audit targets _prospects_ only, and nothing records whether ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity cite the founder's company. Vendors claim that only 12% of URLs cited by AI assistants rank in Google's top 10 ([dupple](https://www.dupple.com/learn/ai-search-visibility-chatgpt-perplexity), unverified), so classic SEO tools cannot stand in.

The cheapest correct move is to **dogfood rather than rebuild**. Beseam already samples ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity and Copilot answers on dated, repeatable runs ([Beseam AI visibility](https://beseam.com/ai-visibility-monitoring)). Trevra can ingest those samples as citation signals for both companies, which keeps the evidence chain intact and makes Beseam a supplier of evidence rather than a feature Trevra clones.

### One LinkedIn identity is a shared single point of failure

The founder's LinkedIn profile (about **4,055 followers**, currently listing LemonCrow as employer while posting about Beseam's category) is the main social asset for _both_ companies ([LinkedIn profile](https://ch.linkedin.com/in/pankaj4u4m)). LinkedIn's User Agreement prohibits using "bots or other unauthorized automated methods to ... send or redirect messages" and lets LinkedIn restrict or terminate accounts ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). On 25 March 2026 LinkedIn removed HeyReach's company page and restricted its executives' personal profiles without stated reason ([HeyReach](https://www.heyreach.io/blog/heyreach-ban)).

Trevra's Companion drives a Chrome profile over CDP, and only one companion device is allowed per workspace ([linkedin-companion.md](../docs/linkedin-companion.md)). Seat state is keyed by `(workspace_id, seat_key)` ([045_linkedin_multi_seat.sql](../migrations/045_linkedin_multi_seat.sql)), so nothing in the schema ties one human profile used in a LemonCrow workspace and a Beseam workspace to a single safety budget. I did not find a cross-workspace guard, and I did not rule one out. If none exists, running both companies from one profile could double its effective volume against bands that were calibrated per seat. Two fixes follow. A founder-level identity budget should span workspaces. A **human-send mode** should let Trevra draft, guard and queue a message that the founder sends manually; LinkedIn's own generative-AI terms ask users to review AI output before sharing ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). This is also why email must become first-class: a founder whose only automated channel is LinkedIn has no fallback if the profile is restricted.

## The 2026 market sells breadth as a stack; governed consolidation is the open lane

The 2026 landscape has split into specialist layers that a founder must assemble: enrichment and orchestration workbenches (Clay, Apollo, Octave), sending infrastructure (Instantly, Smartlead, lemlist), LinkedIn automation (HeyReach, Expandi, Waalaxy), AI SDRs (11x, Artisan, Regie), CRMs with agents (HubSpot Breeze, Attio), signal tools (Common Room, Warmly, RB2B) and enterprise revenue suites (Outreach, Salesloft, Gong).

Clay's Launch plan starts at **$185/month**. CRM integrations, webhooks and the HTTP API need Growth at **$495/month**, and RBAC and SSO are Enterprise-only ([Clay](https://www.clay.com/pricing)). Octave's top tier starts at **$1,500/month** billed annually ([Octave](https://octavehq.com/pricing)). Common Room starts around $20,000 a year ([landbase](https://www.landbase.com/blog/commonroom-pricing), secondary). A minimal founder stack of Clay Launch, Instantly, HeyReach and Attio comes to roughly $400/month before data overages. That is an inference from secondary list prices ([SmartReach](https://smartreach.io/blog/instantly-vs-smartlead-vs-lemlist/), [emelia.io](https://emelia.io/hub/heyreach-pricing), [marketbetter](https://marketbetter.ai/blog/attio-crm-pricing-breakdown-2026/)).

Across the compared products, the inferred table stakes are multi-provider enrichment, signal triggers, email sequencing with managed deliverability, LinkedIn steps, CRM sync, AI drafting, and an MCP or API surface.

Attio's MCP is included on all plans, and Clay and Octave both ship MCP ([Attio MCP](https://attio.com/help/reference/attio-ai/attio-mcp), [Octave](https://octavehq.com/pricing)). Trevra meets signals, LinkedIn, drafting and MCP. It misses enrichment, deliverability and CRM write-back: HubSpot and Attio are read-only plus an approved note, HubSpot scopes exclude `contacts.write`, and there is no Salesforce or Pipedrive adapter ([crm registry](../src/server/crm/registry.ts), [hubspot.ts](../src/server/crm/adapters/hubspot.ts)).

The autonomous end of the market is retreating. Artisan raised $25M in April 2025 on a "Stop Hiring Humans" campaign while still hiring humans ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/09/artisan-the-stop-hiring-humans-ai-agent-startup-raises-25m-and-is-still-hiring-humans)). Competitor blogs claim AI-SDR positive reply rates of **1.3% versus a 2.1% human baseline**, and 50 to 70% churn before renewal ([Kwanzoo](https://www.kwanzoo.com/blog/why-first-gen-ai-sdrs-failed), [Naoma](https://naoma.ai/articles/ai-sdr-dying-2026)). These are unverified vendor claims, but they point the same way as the documented failures: invented capabilities, emails to existing customers, the same pitch to the CTO and the CFO. The tools themselves churn too: Koala shut down on 30 September 2025 after Cursor acqui-hired its team ([Salesmotion](https://salesmotion.io/blog/koala-alternatives)).

None of the verified vendors advertises per-claim provenance or approvals bound to an exact payload. "Approval" means a rep reviewing drafts (HubSpot) or enterprise RBAC (Clay). That gap is Trevra's open lane, but demand for it is inferred, not measured. The strongest counter-argument is that founders buy reply rates, not governance. The answer for these two companies is that governance protects reply rates: a single fabricated benchmark claim would cost LemonCrow more than any sequence gains.

Trevra should therefore not chase Clay's 150-provider waterfall or Apollo's database; Apollo is withheld for ToS reasons ([integration-service.ts](../src/server/integration-service.ts)). It should integrate one bring-your-own-key enrichment provider through Nango, as AGENTS.md requires, show the credit cost in the plan preview, and sell consolidation under one ledger.

## Simplicity comes from fewer surfaces, tiered consent, and sources instead of setup

**Linear** offers the clearest primary evidence for "simple despite breadth": "one really good way of doing things", firm opinions at the atomic level (issue properties), and flexibility only higher up (projects). Its founders openly tolerate mess in peripheral settings so the core flow stays clean ([Figma blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/the-linear-method-opinionated-software/)). **Lightfield**, an AI-native CRM, removes setup entirely: connect mail, calendar and call recorder, and the system assembles the records itself. The claim of under five minutes is a demo claim, not a measurement ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/lightfield-just-assembled-a-working-crm-live-on-stage-then-unstuck-a-stalled-deal-in-3-minutes/)).

The complexity complaints point the same way. Salesforce draws complaints about admin and consultant dependence ([Clevyr](https://clevyr.com/blog/post/how-complex-is-salesforce)). HubSpot's association model becomes a bottleneck as sales complexity grows ([Clevyr](https://clevyr.com/blog/post/salesforce-vs-hubspot-smb)). Clay has a steep learning curve and dual-credit cost unpredictability ([enrich.so](https://enrich.so/blog/clay-review)). All three critiques come from competitor content, so treat them as directional. They share three causes: design work before value, object-model sprawl, and costs metered per action that the user cannot predict.

On approvals the evidence is primary and sobering. Anthropic found Claude Code users **approve 93% of permission prompts**, which leads to "approval fatigue". Its answer is tiering: read-only actions are allowlisted, reviewable edits are allowed, and only risky actions reach a classifier. That classifier still misses **17% of overeager actions** (n=52), and the system escalates to a human after 3 consecutive or 20 total denials ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-auto-mode)). Microsoft's human-AI guidelines open with "make clear what the system can do, and how well" ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/guidelines-for-human-ai-interaction/)). Activation benchmarks are vendor averages. Still, an average activation of **37.5%** and onboarding-checklist completion of just **19.2%** suggest checklists alone do not activate users ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/saas-product-metrics/)).

Trevra's current shape fails several of these tests. The navigation is nominally five sections, but `/research` alone stacks seven: market pulse, stories, distribution outcomes, discovered threads, mentions, company research and a Reddit corpus ([ResearchView.tsx](../src/client/views/ResearchView.tsx), [route.ts](../src/client/ui/route.ts)). The internal simplicity audit found progressive disclosure was **"the exception, not the rule" in 7 of 8 page clusters** ([simplicity audit](../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-19-app-wide-simplicity-audit.md)). The first activation step is "Connect an agent", an MCP-token concept, before any GTM result exists ([activation.ts](../src/client/views/activation.ts)). Reading the documented first run, a LinkedIn-first founder needs roughly six to eight steps across three surfaces plus a terminal before the first real send, and "a signed-in Trevra tab must still remain open when work should run" ([README.md](../README.md)). Exa, YouTube, Meta and other providers switch on through deployment environment variables rather than founder UI ([observations health](../src/server/observations/health.ts)). And the simple path covers too little: **only 3 of 5 intent-compiler objectives can actually prepare work** ([intent.ts](../src/server/gtm/intent.ts)).

The good news is that the mechanisms for simplicity already exist. Policy conditions on side effect, confidence and maximum recipients can express tiered consent today ([policy.ts](../src/server/control-plane/policy.ts)). The Today projection is deterministic and already carries `approval_waiting`, `inbound_submission` and `high_priority_account` items ([today.ts](../src/server/today.ts)). The "not connected, never zero" copy rule already practices honest capability ([gtm-shell-shape.md](../docs/gtm-shell-shape.md)). What is missing is the defaults and the rule that new capability must enter through these channels instead of new screens.

## Conclusion

The exercise changes what "complete" means for Trevra. Two real founder companies show that early-stage pipelines run on product milestones rather than sales stages. The boundary that protects Trevra, "no arbitrary product events", should therefore become "no _unnamed_ product events". Put another way, Trevra's closed execution registry needs a matching closed _observation_ vocabulary. Once that exists, most of the excluded post-sale territory can be covered by observing it rather than owning it, and the product can become fully functional without growing a CRM or a billing system.

The second implication concerns who Trevra actually serves. These two workspaces share one founder, one LinkedIn identity, probably one sending reputation, and one attention budget. Trevra's safety model is calibrated per workspace and per seat, but the scarce, bannable resources belong to the person. Making the _founder_ the unit of safety budgets and of the Today queue across companies is a design choice no competitor's per-seat pricing encourages. It is also where Trevra's deterministic control plane earns its keep. Each added approval prompt makes the remaining ones less likely to be read, so the real test of simplicity is fewer, better approvals, not fewer features.
